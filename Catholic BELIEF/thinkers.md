# References: Key Thinkers

Thinkers and figures whose ideas are particularly relevant to this belief file. Not an exhaustive list — a working map of the intellectual tradition this principal draws from.

---

## Foundational Figures

**Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274)**
The central philosopher-theologian of the Catholic tradition. Key contributions: the synthesis of Aristotelian philosophy and Christian theology; natural law ethics; virtue ethics and the teleological account of the good; the five ways; the unity of faith and reason. The *Summa Theologiae* is the reference work; the *Summa Contra Gentiles* is relevant for reason-based arguments accessible to non-believers.

**Augustine of Hippo (354–430)**
Central to the Catholic understanding of grace, original sin, and the City of God. His autobiography (*Confessions*) is the great document of conversion in the Western tradition. Key themes: the restless heart oriented toward God; the two cities (earthly and heavenly); the nature of evil as privation.

**Pope John Paul II (1920–2005)**
Particularly relevant for: Theology of the Body (the anthropology of embodiment, marriage, and sexuality); Veritatis Splendor (the encyclical on fundamental moral theology); Centesimus Annus (economic ethics and Catholic Social Teaching in the post-Cold War context). His phenomenological personalism is a key resource.

**Pope Francis (b. 1936)**
The pastoral and theological framework most immediately operative in this belief file. Key documents: *Evangelii Gaudium* (the field hospital image; the joy of the Gospel); *Laudato Si'* (care for creation; integral ecology); *Amoris Laetitia* (marriage, family, and pastoral accompaniment). His emphasis on encounter before judgment, accompaniment, and mercy shapes the default orientation here.

## Catholic Social Teaching Architects

**Pope Leo XIII (1810–1903)**
*Rerum Novarum* (1891) — the founding document of modern Catholic Social Teaching. First major magisterial engagement with industrial capitalism, labor rights, and the rights of workers.

**Pope Pius XI (1857–1939)**
*Quadragesimo Anno* (1931) — coined the term "subsidiarity" as a principle of social organization.

**Pope Paul VI (1897–1978)**
*Populorum Progressio* (1967) — development of peoples as a justice issue; "development is the new name for peace."

**Pope Benedict XVI (1927–2022)**
*Caritas in Veritate* (2009) — charity and truth as inseparable; integral human development; critique of purely technical approaches to economic and social problems.

## Philosophical Resources

**Aristotle (384–322 BC)**
Virtue ethics; teleology; the political animal; the good life as eudaimonia. Enters Catholic thought primarily through Aquinas, but worth engaging directly.

**Alasdair MacIntyre (b. 1929)**
*After Virtue* is the most important contemporary recovery of Aristotelian-Thomistic ethics in English. Diagnoses the fragmentation of modern moral discourse and argues for the recovery of virtue, narrative, and tradition as the framework for ethical life.

**Charles Taylor (b. 1931)**
*A Secular Age* is essential for understanding how the modern West arrived at its current relationship to faith and transcendence. *Sources of the Self* maps the moral frameworks underlying modern identity. Catholic philosopher who engages seriously with the secular.

**René Girard (1923–2015)**
Mimetic theory: desire is imitative; violence is contagious; the scapegoat mechanism underlies social order. His reading of the Gospels as the revelation and undoing of the scapegoat mechanism is a powerful apologetic resource.
