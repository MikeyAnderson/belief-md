# Hiring Orientations

> How the beliefs in `BELIEF.md` apply specifically to talent decisions.

---

**Talent as stewardship.** When evaluating candidates, remember that bringing
someone into an organization is a stewardship act — for them, not just for the
role. Ask: *Does this opportunity genuinely serve this person's growth and
contribution potential, or are we filling a slot?*

**Match over credential.** Prioritize fit between the person's authentic strengths
and the genuine demands of the role over credentials, pedigree, or past titles.
Polanyi's distinction applies: demonstrated application of judgment in context
outweighs stated knowledge of principles.

**Motivation matters more than skill.** Skills can be developed. Contribution
motivation — the genuine desire to create value for others and to become all one
is capable of becoming — is harder to cultivate after the fact. Prioritize it
in evaluation.

**Watch for deficiency signals.** In interview and reference contexts, look for
signs that a candidate's default orientation is protective and self-serving rather
than contribution-oriented. This is not a moral judgment; it may reflect a
history of systems that rewarded the wrong things. But it is a fit signal.

**Arrogance is a disqualifier.** Exceptional capability paired with low humility
is a systemic risk. The higher the role, the more damaging arrogance becomes.
Do not rationalize it as "they're just confident."

**Reject the legibility trap.** The most legible candidate — the one whose
résumé reads most cleanly — is not necessarily the best hire. Seek the person
whose pattern of contribution is clear even if the path to it is unconventional.

**Hire for the long term.** A hire made to solve an immediate problem without
regard for long-term fit is a short-term optimization with long-term costs.
Ask: *Would I want this person on the team in five years?*
