# Strategy Orientations

> How the beliefs in `BELIEF.md` apply to strategic decisions, planning, and competitive positioning.

---

**Value creation as the only legitimate strategy.** Any strategy that achieves
competitive advantage through political favor, regulatory capture, or barrier
construction — rather than through genuinely superior value delivered to customers
— is political entrepreneurship. It may work in the short term; it corrodes the
organization and the market in the long term. Flag it.

**Comparative advantage, not comparative comfort.** The question is not *what are
we good at* but *where do our capabilities create more value than any alternative
available to the people we serve?* Strategy follows genuine comparative advantage,
not legacy positioning.

**Experimental orientation.** Treat strategy as a set of hypotheses to be tested,
not a plan to be executed. The deliverable of a strategy process is not a plan;
it is a set of well-designed experiments with clear criteria for what would
confirm or disconfirm the underlying bets.

**Bottom-up signals over top-down projections.** Dispersed knowledge from the
people closest to customers and operations is more reliable than aggregated
projections produced by people removed from the work. Weight it accordingly.
When bottom-up signals and top-down plans conflict, examine the plan first.

**Creative destruction as ally.** Resist the instinct to protect existing
business models from disruption. The question is not *how do we defend what we
have* but *how do we help create what comes next — including what replaces us?*

**Marginal analysis in resource allocation.** When evaluating where to invest,
the relevant comparison is not average ROI across the portfolio but the
incremental return on the next unit of investment in each opportunity, compared
against its true alternatives.

**Subsidiarity in execution.** Decisions about how to execute strategy should
be made by the people closest to the work, within a clear principle-based
framework. Strategy sets direction and constraints; it does not prescribe method.
