## Principle-Based Management (PBM)

Developed by Charles Koch and refined over decades at Koch Industries. PBM is
a comprehensive management philosophy grounded in the observation that the same
principles that allow free societies to flourish can be applied within
organizations to produce superior, principled performance.

**The Five Dimensions of PBM:**

1. **Vision** — Determining where and how the organization can create the most
   value. Not just a goal statement; a principled theory of how this organization
   creates value that others cannot.

2. **Virtue and Talents** — Selecting people with the values, skills, and
   capabilities to create value for customers and society. Virtue precedes talent
   in the framework — a person with great talent and low integrity is a liability.

3. **Knowledge Processes** — Creating, acquiring, sharing, and applying relevant
   knowledge — and eliminating inefficiencies that arise from poor information.
   Includes the epistemic culture of challenge and falsification.

4. **Decision Rights** — Ensuring the right people make the right decisions.
   Decisions should be made by those with the best knowledge, the right incentives,
   and the appropriate accountability. Centralizing decisions beyond this optimum
   destroys value.

5. **Incentives** — Rewarding people for creating real value. Incentive systems
   that reward activity, tenure, or compliance rather than genuine contribution
   undermine everything else.

**Key distinctions in PBM:**
- *Principled Entrepreneurship* vs. *political entrepreneurship*
- *Contribution motivation* vs. *deficiency motivation*
- *Conceptual knowledge* vs. *personal knowledge*
- *Value creation* vs. *value capture*

**Primary sources:**
- *Good Profit* — Charles Koch (2015)
- *Believe in People* — Charles Koch (2020)
- principlebasedmanagement.com

## Market-Based Management® (MBM®)

The original trademarked name for the framework developed at Koch Industries,
now referred to as PBM in public-facing materials. The core insight: the
mechanisms that make free markets productive — price signals, competition,
property rights, voluntary exchange, rule of law — can be internalized within
organizations through structure, culture, and incentive design.

## Popperian Epistemology

Applied epistemological framework derived from Karl Popper's philosophy of
science. Core operating principles:

- All knowledge is provisional; any claim should be held open to falsification
- The mark of a genuine empirical claim is that it can, in principle, be proven
  wrong — and tests are designed to attempt this
- Theories that survive genuine attempts at falsification earn credence
- Theories insulated from challenge by definition or institutional protection
  are not knowledge; they are dogma

Applied in PBM as the epistemic culture of challenge: any claim, plan, or
recommendation should be subjected to the best available disconfirming evidence
before adoption.

## Catallaxy (Hayek)

Hayek's term for the spontaneous order that emerges from voluntary exchange among
people acting on their own dispersed knowledge. Distinct from an "economy" (which
implies central design); a catallaxy is an emergent order, not a designed one.

Implication: the best organizational structures are not those that optimize a
designed plan but those that create conditions for individuals to apply their
dispersed knowledge in value-creating ways — within a principle-based framework
that ensures voluntary, mutually beneficial exchange.
