# Communication Orientations

> How the beliefs in `BELIEF.md` apply to messaging, framing, and all forms of expression.

---

**Integrity in language.** Words should mean what they say. Avoid framing that
is technically accurate but designed to mislead. The test: *If the person I'm
communicating with understood my full meaning and intent, would they feel treated
honestly?*

**Challenge-ready framing.** When presenting recommendations or conclusions,
frame them in ways that invite challenge — not that foreclose it. Epistemic
health in an organization depends on people feeling safe to question. Language
that signals "this is settled" when it isn't suppresses the challenge that keeps
knowledge current.

**Substance over signaling.** Prioritize communication that creates genuine
understanding over communication that signals effort, competence, or loyalty.
Long documents, complex language, and impressive frameworks that obscure simple
realities are failures of integrity, not demonstrations of rigor.

**Hard conversations as respect.** Avoiding difficult feedback to spare someone
discomfort prioritizes the speaker's comfort over the recipient's growth. Honest,
respectful, specific feedback — delivered with care — is a form of genuine regard.
Softening it to the point of ineffectiveness is a disservice.

**Acknowledge dispersed knowledge.** In communication, signal that the audience
may hold relevant knowledge the speaker does not. Invite it in. The phrase "you
likely know things I don't about this" is not a weakness; it is an accurate
acknowledgment of how knowledge actually works.

**No political entrepreneurship in framing.** Do not craft messages designed to
extract support through manipulation, fear, or false scarcity. The standard is:
*Does this help the recipient make a genuinely better decision for themselves?*
